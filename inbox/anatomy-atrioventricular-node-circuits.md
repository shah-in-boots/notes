The AV node itself is a complex structure that generally sits in the Triangle of Koch.
The compact AV node itself sits above the the Tendon of Todaro with the His bundle continuing in a more superior-anterior position.
The AV node itself has multiple extensions, including the fast pathway, and the anisotropic tissue tha tmakes up the leftward and rightward inferior extensions (**LIE** & **RIE**), which are putatively the *slow pathway(s)*. 

# Fast AV nodal pathway

The putative fast AV nodal pathway has specific properties...

- Shortest conduction time
- Formed by transitional cells that cross the tendon of Todaro superiorly/posteriorly
- Earliest retrograde atrial activation $\rightarrow$ occurs on the bilateral interatrial septum, posterior to the tendon of Todaro and $\frac{1}{3}$ the distance between the His bundle and the CS roof 
	- for example, the CS 7-8 is likely earlier than CS 9-10

# Slow AV nodal pathway(s)

The __rightward inferior extension__ is the most common area that we work on, the putative slow pathway...

- Longest conduction time
- Earliest retrograde atrial activation $\rightarrow$ occurs between tA and CS os

The __leftward inferior extension__  exists towards the LA, and is less common the culprit in arrhythmias...

- Conduction time is shorter than the rightward extensions
- Earliest retrograde atrial activation $\rightarrow$ is roof of CS about 2-4 cm from CS ostia

The __left inferolateral extension__ is even less common...

- Extends almost to the mid-LA
- Composed of similar anisotropic tissue

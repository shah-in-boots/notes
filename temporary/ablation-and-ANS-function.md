In 2025 to 2026 time range, abltion in AF has switched predominantly to PFA, which is theoretically cardioselective and should spare permanent injury to neuronal cells (e.g. phrenic nerve, GPs, etc).

RF however had direct GP effect that could be more durable. 

@Ferreira2024 studied this over a 6 month period with repeat ANS testing after thermal ablation (both cryo-ablation and RF-ablation). 
The study was on n $\approx$ 80 with AF who presented in sinus, but without ANS altering disease processes or medications (including beta blockers).
AFEQT, tilt table,, handgrip (sympathetic testing), and deep breathing (parasympathetic) testing were performed pre-ablation, at 1 month, 3 month, and 6 month follow-up.

![figures/Ferreira2024/fig-7-x161-y351.png](figures/Ferreira2024/fig-7-x161-y351.png)

The most prominent finding was change in heart rate over time.
Procedurally, the GPs were not intentionally targeted.

Autosomal dominant $LOF$ of *SCN5A*, although more common in males, particularly Southeast Asian men in terms of cause of SCD.
20% of cases have *SCN5A* mutations.
Sudden death occurs at rest or at night.
ECG with J-point elevation in V1 through V3, with RBBB pattern.

Worsened by $Na^+$ blockade (procainamide in the US, ajmaline in Europe) and beta-blockers. 
Improved with isoproterenol and exercise. 
The ECG pattern is sporadic and dynamic, worsens with fever

If imaging is performed, will see LGE in regions associated with ECG changes

Many subtypes of Brugada syndrome are in the literature, however the evidence is predominantly for *SCN5A* mutations leading to dysfunction of the $Na_v1.5$ protein.

# ECGs

When obtaining an ECG there are three general patterns.

1. Type 1 shows RBBB with coved ST segment and inverted T waves ("sharkfin")
1. Type 2 shows similar RBBB morphology with ST elevation > 2 mm and saddleback T wave
1. Type 3 is similar to type 2, except ST elevations are < 2 mm

# Conduction

Conduction velocity is partially determined by the *source-sink* mismatch, like in situation of accessory pathways.
- transition of Purkinje fibers into ventricular myocardium is a small source leading to a large sink, which would go with a slower conduction velocity
- transition of ventricular myocardium (or atrial) into an accessory pathway is hte opposite, where the source is much larger than the sink, leading to faster conduction and less changes of block in that direction

![$\theta_0$ is the steady state conduction velocity of a planar wavefront, and the diagram demonstrates how the conduction velocity would change depending on shape of the wavefront (e.g. convex or concave)](resources/cellular-electrophysiology-conduction-of-wavefronts.png)

## Tissue type

Slow response tissue is usually mediated through $Ca^{++}$, like the AV node, and will show more delay with premature beats, as it allows for decrement.
-  $Ca^{++}$ dependent for upstroke current
- Slow conduction with decremental properties
- Recovery from inactivation is time-dependent
- SA node and AV node

Fast response tissue, like myocardial cells, have an *all-or-none* response, and thus will conduct at a similar rate until a threshold is met (and then will fail to conduct altogether) $\rightarrow$ non-decremental
- $Na^+$ is primarily responsible for upstroke current
- Fast response is *all-or-nothing*
- Recovery from inactivation is voltage dependent
- Myocardial cells (e.g. atria, ventricle), His-Purkinje system

## Conduction velocity determinants

Contributors to conduction velocity are partially anatomical.
Filament/fiber direction matters, such that going along the tubule is faster than across.
That may be mediated by the fact that $Na^+$ current concentrates at the end of cells, as well as gap junctions (formed from connexons), described in  [cellular-electrophysiology-channels](permanent/cellular-electrophysiology-channels.md).
Most common connexons are Cx43, Cx40, and Cx45. 
Because of that, side-to-side is is 2-3 times slower than end-to-end conduction.

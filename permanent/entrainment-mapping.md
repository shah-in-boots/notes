# Ventricular tachycardia

Entrainment mapping is probably most commonly used approach for ischemic VT.
Key concept is progressive and stable fusion from pacing, which allows you to know if an arrhythmia is reentrant versus focal.

Here is an example of a focal VT and reentrant VT differences with pacing.

![Example of VOP that leads to suppression without fusion, as the paced morphology during VT is similar to paced morphology during sinus rhythm.](resources/entrainment-mapping.png)

![The VOP here shows progressive fusion at increasingly faster PCL, merging between that of the VT and the purely paced morphology](resources/entrainment-mapping-1.png)

Goal of entrainment mapping is to identify the parts of the VT circuit, moving closer and closer to the critical isthmus, going from manifest fusion to concealed fusion with pacing.

Common patterns include...

- Protected isthmus has diastolic activation, with concealed fusion and $PPI = TCL$
- Adjacent bystander has diastolic activation but $PPI > TCL$
- Outer loop has manifest fusion with $PPI = TCL$
- Remote bystander has manifest fusion with $PPI > TCL$

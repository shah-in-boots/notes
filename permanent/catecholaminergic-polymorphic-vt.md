# Catecholaminergic polymorphic VT (CPVT)

Autosomal dominant type associated with mutations in the ryanodine release channel in the sarcoplasmic reticulum, *RYR2*.
Leads to an exercise-related episodes of bidirectional VT
Treated with good effect by beta-blockers and flecainide 
Autosomal recessive form seen with mutations in calsequestrin

# Mechanism

Helpful diagram seen in [cellular-electrophysiology-currents](permanent/cellular-electrophysiology-currents.md)

![Neutral schematic ov major cellular currents](resources/cellular-electrophysiology-14.png)

$Ca^{++}$ normally is released from sarcoplasmic reticilum (SR) through the *RYR2* channels, leading to depolarization/contraction.
In CPVT, the *RYR2* channels are leaking, leading to increased cytosolic $Ca^{++}$, which is then removed through NCX ($\downarrow Na^+ \times 3 = \uparrow Ca^{++} \times 1$), which leads to delayed after-depolarizations (DADs) that trigger AP and arrhythmias.

[arrhythmia-mechanisms](permanent/arrhythmia-mechanisms.md) contains more description of DADs.

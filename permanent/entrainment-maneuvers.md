__Entrainment__ is helpful to help define the mechanism of tachycardia and choose ablation sites.

- In automaticity, triggered activity, or microreentry, the lack of fusion can give us a sense that this is a focal location (and not a large circuit), and thus the appropriate ablation site will have *late diastolic timing*
- In macroreentry, can determine or validate distance from circuit, the amount of fusion, and assess diastolic potentials, witsh ablation site being in critical isthmus region with *mid-diastolic timing*

Key literature includes: 
- @Henthorn1988
- @Waldo1977 
- @Vollmann2012

# Definition

Concept developed by Al Waldo, in @Waldo1977. 
Entrainment requires....
1. Continuous resetting of a reentry circuit
1. Continuation of tachycardia after pacing, *critical for mapping*
1. Proven by...
	- constant fusion
	- progressive fusion
	- characteristic termination with evidence of conduction block

Entrainment is a major part of [scar-mediated-ventricular-tachycardia](permanent/scar-mediated-ventricular-tachycardia.md)


What is __entrainment__?

- Continuous tachycardia resetting by overdrive pacing
- Requires a stable, ongoing tachycardia, where stability $\rightarrow$ $\Delta TCL < 30\ ms$ 
- Overdrive pacing at a fixed rate slightly faster than tachycardia
- Same tachycardia resumes on cessation of pacing
- Fusion occurs with pacing
	- *fixed* fusion is stable blend of pacing and underlying tachycardia
	- *progressive* fusion shows greater contribution of pacing to fused complexes when PCL increases from the same site (larger proportion due to pacing)
	- *concealed* fusion may occur with microreentry

What is __fusion__?

When attempting entrainment, fusion is required (as above) to confirm.
Fusion is *NOT* ... 

- Fusion is not just capture with overdrive pacing
- Overdrive pacing followed by change in tachcyardia or termination

To confirm fusion, can use multiple factors to asses. 
Fusion *IS*...

- Clear blend of paced complex and tachycardia complex
- Stimulus occurs after an acelerated complex
- Graded changes in the paced complex occur at different PCL, consistent with *progressive fusion*
- Pacing *downstream* should accelerate *upstream* EGMs

*Fully paced complexes can be verified by knowing what the morphology should look like, but can check by pacing during sinus or pacing very rapidly during tachycardia (to shift majority proportion of the morphology to the paced complex).*

![The downstream-upstream effect seen here. Pacing stimuli comes after onset of atrial complex and accelerates the next *upstream* component (to PCL) with post-pacing downstream component being at TCL.](resources/entrainment-maneuvers.png)

Hints for entrainment...

- Consistent capture is present prior to evaluating
- Pace long enough to ensure entrainment, $\uparrow$ pacing duration when $\Delta_{PCL - TCL}$ is small
- Pace slow enough to entrain, too fast will terminate
- More recording sites available, easier to see fusion (e.g. well-spaced intracardiac electrodes for AT, surface 12-lead ECG for VT)
- Check EGMs from far away from pacing site as a control
- Further you pace from the arrhythmia exit, easier it is to show fusion (and harder to to entrain)
- Pacing from several sites at outset (during sinus rhythm) as baseline of fully-paced beats
- Easier to show *presence* of fusion than *absence*

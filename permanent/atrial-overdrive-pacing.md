Atrial overdrive pacing (**AOP**) during SVT is similar to ventricular overdrive pacing (**VOP**) from a practical perspective. 
More details on VOP at [ventricular-overdrive-pacing](permanent/ventricular-overdrive-pacing.md). 

AOP can have variable responses, based on underlying mechanism

- Fusion present
	- assumes that can be entrained
	- diagnoses as a reentrant rhythm, most likely macroreentry
	- $PPI - TCL$ is the distance from pacing site to the circuit (including the *there-and-back* distance)
- Fusion absent (trial multiple sites and rates)
	- transient suppression with subsequent warm-up $\rightarrow$ automatic tachycardia
	- transient acceleration $\rightarrow$ suggests DAD-related triggered activity
	- fixed return cycle length regardless of pacing suggests microreentry or concealed fusion in macroreentry
	- $PPI - TCL$ is distance from pacing site to focus (not a circuit necessarily)
- $AHHA$ response = junctional tachycardia
- $AHA$ response = AT, AVNRT, ORT

Fusion is dependent on entrainment and has specific definition, explored further in [entrainment-maneuvers](permanent/entrainment-maneuvers.md)

Additional things can occur with overdrive pacing, such as termination without propagation. Details in [termination-without-global-capture](permanent/termination-without-global-capture.md)

# SVT patterns with AOP

[atrioventricular-nodal-reentrant-tachycardia](inbox/atrioventricular-nodal-reentrant-tachycardia.md)

In typical slow-fast AVNRT, AOP will collide antidromically with retrograde fast pathway activation, and accelerate anterograde slow pathway activation.
When releasing AOP, the response will then allow for retrograde fast pathway to activate the atria, giving an $AHVA$ or $AHA$ response.
This response pattern rules out junctional tachycardia.

[junctional-tachycardia](inbox/junctional-tachycardia.md)

During junctional tachycardia, the AOP response would be $AHVHVA$, or more simply $AHHA$, due to automaticity of the junction restarting after overdrive pacing resolves.

![figures/Venkatachalam2011a/fig-2-x46-y565.png](figures/Venkatachalam2011a/fig-2-x46-y565.png)


*Amplifier saturation versus signal clipping. At higher gains, the output signal amplitudes try to exceed the power supply voltage of the amplifier, causing saturation of the amplifier with delayed recovery. Clipping artificially cuts off the signal above a predetermined amplitude, without the delayed recovery, since the amplifier is still operating in its linear range. Also shown are His1 and His2 signals being limited in amplitude by clipping (right panel). Note that His3 and His4 are unchanged with and without clipping (left and right panels) since their signal amplitudes are below the level of the clipping plane of the system.*

![figures/Venkatachalam2011a/fig-3-x88-y102.png](figures/Venkatachalam2011a/fig-3-x88-y102.png)


*A, Phantom interference (pickup) by the His1 channel during pacing from the right ventricular (RV) catheter. The stimulator is attempting to pace the RV at a cycle length of 500 m, but the pacing function has not been enabled on the data acquisition system. Pacing spikes are capacitively coupled to the His1 channel and manifest as small potentials not synchronous with the intrinsic rhythm (arrows). B, Phantom pickup on the ABLd (catheter in left atrium) with an unconnected ABLd. The unconnected ABLd amplifier input picks up a far-field ventricular signal, probably from adjacent signals on the connector block. The ABLd signal (proximal electrode pair on the same catheter) is partially within the long sheath and shows increased noise, although the pickup is slightly visible faintly.*

*A, Noise on the ABLd electrode during radiofrequency ablation with and without the pacing function enabled on the ABLd. (Coronary sinus pacing is being performed.) The noise goes down substantially when the pacing function on the ABLd is disabled, eliminating the imbalance on the ABLd electrodes and improving noise rejection. B, The baseline noise on the coronary sinus 3,4 electrode in the lower image is also eliminated when the pacing function on that electrode pair is disabled. (No pacing is being performed in this recording.)*
